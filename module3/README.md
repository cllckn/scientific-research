## 1. Overview

A scientific research manuscript in Information Technology follows a structured format to ensure clarity, reproducibility, transparency, and logical presentation of contributions. This structure enables researchers, reviewers, and practitioners to understand the problem, methodology, experimental design, results, and scientific contribution.

The typical structure of an IT research manuscript includes:

1. Title
2. Abstract
3. Keywords
4. Introduction
5. Related Work / Literature Review
6. Methodology / Proposed System or Approach
7. Experimental Setup and Evaluation Methodology
8. Results and Discussion
9. Conclusion and Future Work
10. References

---

## 2. Title

### Purpose
The title provides a concise and precise description of the research contribution.

### Characteristics
- Clear and specific
- Reflects the main contribution or experimental focus
- Avoids vague terms (e.g., “A Study of…” unless justified)
- Includes key technical concepts

### Example
- *Performance Evaluation of a RESTful Microservice Architecture Using Load Testing*
- *Comparative Analysis of Logistic Regression and SVM for Iris Dataset Classification*

---

## 3. Abstract

### Purpose
The abstract summarizes the entire paper in a single paragraph (typically 150–250 words).

### Structure of a Good Abstract
- Problem statement
- Research objective
- Methodology
- Experimental setup
- Key results
- Main conclusion

### Important Notes
- No citations
- No undefined abbreviations
- Must stand alone

---

## 4. Keywords

### Purpose
Keywords improve discoverability in digital libraries and indexing databases.

### Guidelines
- 4–6 keywords
- Specific technical terms
- Avoid overly general words

### Example
- RESTful API
- Performance evaluation
- Load testing
- Machine learning
- Statistical analysis

---

## 5. Introduction

### Purpose
Introduces the research problem and motivates the study.

### Typical Structure
1. Background context
2. Problem statement
3. Research gap
4. Research objective
5. Contributions of the paper
6. Paper organization (optional)

### Key Questions to Address
- Why is this problem important?
- What is missing in existing research?
- What does this paper contribute?

---

## 6. Related Work / Literature Review

### Purpose
Positions the research within existing scientific literature.

### Objectives
- Summarize relevant prior studies
- Compare methodologies and findings
- Identify research gaps
- Justify the novelty of the proposed work

### Best Practices
- Use reputable sources (journals, conferences)
- Avoid simple summaries — provide critical comparison
- Group studies by themes or methods

---

## 7. Methodology / Proposed System or Approach

### Purpose
Describes how the research problem is addressed.

### In IT Research, This May Include:
- System architecture
- Algorithm design
- RESTful service design
- Repository pattern implementation
- Data processing workflow
- Mathematical formulation (if applicable)

### Requirements
- Clear diagrams (architecture diagrams, flowcharts)
- Precise technical details
- Sufficient detail for reproducibility

---

## 8. Experimental Setup and Evaluation Methodology

### Purpose
Explains how experiments were designed and conducted.

### Must Include
- Hardware and software environment
- Dataset description (e.g., Iris dataset)
- Performance metrics:
    - Latency (average, P50, P95, P99)
    - Throughput (RPS)
    - Accuracy, precision, recall (for ML)
- Hypotheses
- Controlled variables
- Number of repetitions
- Statistical testing method (e.g., t-test)

### Key Principle
The experiment must be reproducible.

---

## 9. Results and Discussion

### Purpose
Presents and interprets experimental findings.

### Components
- Tables and graphs
- Statistical outputs
- Comparison between approaches
- Interpretation of performance trade-offs

### Discussion Should:
- Explain why results occurred
- Connect findings to hypotheses
- Compare results with related work
- Identify limitations

Avoid:
- Repeating raw numbers without analysis
- Making unsupported claims

---

## 10. Conclusion and Future Work

### Purpose
Summarizes contributions and outlines research extensions.

### Should Include
- Restatement of the research objective
- Summary of main findings
- Confirmation or rejection of hypotheses
- Practical implications
- Limitations
- Suggestions for future research

Avoid introducing new data or references.

---

## 11. References

### Purpose
Provides full citation of all referenced works.

### Requirements
- Follow a consistent citation style (IEEE, ACM, APA, etc.)
- Cite only credible scientific sources
- Ensure in-text citations match reference list
- Avoid plagiarism

### Tools
- Mendeley
- BibTeX with LaTeX
- Overleaf reference management

---

# Logical Flow of an IT Research Manuscript

Problem → Literature Gap → Proposed Method → Experimental Design → Results → Interpretation → Contribution
